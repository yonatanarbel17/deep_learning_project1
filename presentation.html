Overcoming the Main Challenge

"Our biggest challenge wasn't the model — it was preventing data leakage and building a representative validation set."

❌ The Problem (Run 1)

  • Game-level split: last 2 games → validation
  • Only 200 val samples from 2 games
  • Augmented variants could leak across splits
  • Result: 23-point overfit gap

✓ The Solution (Run 2+)

  • Frame-group split: group by (game_id, frame)
  • All augmented variants stay together → no leakage
  • 595 val samples from 9 games
  • Result: val ≥ train (no overfitting)

Why EfficientNet-B2?

  • Compound scaling optimizes depth, width & resolution together
  • Best accuracy-to-parameter ratio for our data size
  • Outperformed ResNet18 by +5.96%
  • Outperformed larger models (Swin, ResNet50) which overfit

Loss Function

  • CrossEntropy with label smoothing (α=0.1)
  • Inverse-frequency class weights
  • Rare pieces (queens, kings) weighted higher
  • Smoothing prevents overconfident predictions

Training Config

  • AdamW optimizer, weight decay 1e-3
  • ReduceLROnPlateau (patience=3)
  • Gradient clipping (max_norm=1.0)
  • WeightedRandomSampler for class balance

What Didn't Work

✕ ResNet18 / ResNet50 / Swin Transformer

ResNet18 lacked capacity. ResNet50 and Swin overfit on our small dataset. EfficientNet-B2 was the sweet spot between capacity and generalization.

✕ Board-Level Transformer Context

2-layer TransformerEncoder letting squares attend to each other. Added complexity without improving accuracy — the 70% overlap padding already provides sufficient spatial context.

✕ Full Fine-Tuning from Epoch 1

Without any freezing, the model memorized training data immediately. 95% train vs 72% val — catastrophic forgetting destroyed ImageNet features.

✕ Heavy Online Augmentation

Strong ColorJitter (0.3/0.3) was redundant with offline variants, creating unrealistic training images. Reducing to 0.1/0.1 improved results.

✕ Higher Dropout (0.4)

Excessive regularization reduced model capacity unnecessarily. Dropout 0.3 was the right balance for our data size.

✕ Game-Level Validation Split

Splitting by game gave only 200 unrepresentative samples from 2 games. The model appeared to generalize but actually had data leakage through augmented variants.

Key Takeaways

1

Data Quality > Model Complexity

Fixing the data split and augmentation strategy yielded far bigger gains than any architectural change. Offline augmentation alone contributed +7.25%.

2

Gradual Unfreezing for Small Datasets

Stabilizing the head before fine-tuning the backbone was our most impactful technique. It prevents catastrophic forgetting while enabling domain adaptation (+4.00%).

3

Right-Sized Models Win

EfficientNet-B2 outperformed both smaller (ResNet18, −5.96%) and larger (Swin, ResNet50) alternatives. With limited data, the right inductive bias matters more than capacity.

4

Domain Knowledge as Free Constraints

Chess rules (one king, no pawns on rank 1/8, piece limits) are guaranteed constraints that catch errors the model can't learn from limited data — essentially free accuracy.
